\section{The Substrate: What Actually Breaks, Measured}
\label{sec:substrate}

A correctness ladder cannot be built on a foundation that is itself
language-dependent. Before NEPANTLA can be stated, the deterministic control
plane must be made language-\emph{neutral} --- and the first step is to
establish, by measurement rather than assertion, exactly how it currently
behaves.

Every result in this section was obtained by executing the real functions against
the real registries.

\subsection{The tokenizer destroys Spanish before any logic runs}
\label{sec:tokenizer}

The single tokenizer used by every scoring path is

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python,caption={The single tokenizer shared by every scoring path.},label={lst:token-regex}]
_TOKEN_RE = re.compile(r"[a-z0-9_]+")     # capability_registry.py:20
\end{lstlisting}

Applied after lowercasing, every non-ASCII byte is a \textbf{delimiter}, and
single-character fragments are discarded:

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\small
\caption{Executed tokenizer output for accented Spanish input. Every non-ASCII
byte acts as a delimiter, so the surviving fragments can never equal an English
hint token.}
\label{tab:tokenizer-fragments}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Input} & \textbf{Tokens produced today} \\
\midrule
\code{código}        & \code{['digo']} \\
\code{análisis}      & \code{['lisis']} \\
\code{ejecución}     & \code{['ejecuci']} \\
\code{contraseña}    & \code{['contrase']} \\
\code{años}          & \code{['os']} \\
\code{envía}         & \code{['env']} \\
\code{configuración} & \code{['configuraci']} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Three consequences. The token-overlap term of the capability scorer (worth up to
$+10$) is \textbf{structurally zero} for accented Spanish, because a fragment can
never equal an English hint token. Behaviour becomes \textbf{non-deterministic
across keyboards} --- \code{ejecucion} tokenizes to \code{['ejecucion']} but
\code{ejecución} to \code{['ejecuci']}, so identical intent scores differently
depending on whether the accent was typed. And the defect propagates: the
execution planner and the Multi-Turn tool-budget selector both import this
tokenizer, so one line compromises three independent consumers.

\label{sec:tool-selection}

matched prompt pairs:

The English scores are healthy (\code{send\_email} 44, \code{shoter} 34,
\code{summarize} 32, \code{grepper} 32). The Spanish behaviour is worse than weak
--- it is \textbf{adversarial}, because phrase matching is unbounded substring
containment rather than word-boundary matching, and 107 registry alias/hint
phrases are four characters or shorter.

The canonical demonstration: for
\foreignlanguage{spanish}{``envía un correo de prueba a soporte''} (``send a test
email to support''), \code{chat\_agent\_unrealer} scores \textbf{24 and ranks
first}, because its alias \code{ue} and its hint \code{ue} both match inside
\textbf{prue}ba ($+12$, $+10$). The semantically correct
\code{chat\_agent\_send\_email} scores \textbf{0} and does not appear in the
positive list at all. The substring \code{ue} occurs in \emph{que, puede, fue,
aunque, muestra, respuesta, nuevo, bueno} --- a large fraction of all Spanish
sentences --- making Unrealer a permanent phantom top hit; it took top-1 in 4 of
the 12 Spanish cases.

Other verified collisions: \code{uno}/\code{ino} (Arduiner) inside \emph{uno,
camino, destino, término}; \code{pio} (ESP32er) inside \emph{limpio, propio,
principio}; \code{pod} (Kuberneter) inside \emph{poder, podemos}; \code{pid} and
\code{api} (PSer, Apirer) both inside \emph{rápido}; \code{ls} (Globber) inside
\emph{falso, pulsar}; \code{git} (Gitter) inside \emph{digital, legítimo};
\code{spa} (Playwrighter) inside \emph{espacio, España}. And a subtler one: the
Spanish word \emph{imagen} \textbf{contains} the English \code{image}, a hint for
both Image-Interpreter and Dockerer; they tie at 10, and because the tie-break is
\emph{registry index}, \textbf{Dockerer outranks Image-Interpreter on
\foreignlanguage{spanish}{``interpreta la imagen''}}.

Compounding this, the 37-word stopword list is English-only, so Spanish function
words survive into the token set. Since \code{chat\_agent\_de\_compresser} splits
into tokens \code{('chat','agent','de','compresser')}, the Spanish preposition
\textbf{\code{de} awards $+2$ to De-Compresser on essentially every Spanish
sentence.}

\subsection{The planner then fails closed}
\label{sec:planner-fails-closed}

The two consumers of the score disagree on polarity. The standalone selector
fails \textbf{open} (returns the full tool list when nothing scores). The
execution planner fails \textbf{closed}: when nothing crosses threshold it emits
empty tuples plus the note \emph{``No tool or agent capability crossed the
planner threshold''}, sets \code{execution\_mode='direct\_model'}, and injects the
summary line \textbf{``Selected tools/agents: none''} into the system prompt.

Verified: the Spanish prompt \foreignlanguage{spanish}{``Borra los archivos
temporales de esa carpeta''} produces zero positive-scoring capabilities, so the
planner explicitly informs the model that no tool stage is needed --- \textbf{for
a destructive file operation.}

A second-order effect follows. When the full tool surface exceeds the context
budget, bind order is decided by the same scorer; under Spanish input the planner
set is empty and virtually every score is zero, so the sort key degenerates to
\code{(0, 0, name)} and the surviving non-core tools are selected
\textbf{alphabetically}.

\subsection{A Spanish conjunction corrupts file paths today}
\label{sec:conjunction-corruption}

The wrapped-agent argument parser recognizes exactly two natural-language
separators:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python,caption={The wrapped-agent conjunction rule: two English separators, no others.},label={lst:conjunction-regex}]
_CONJUNCTION_ASSIGNMENT_RE = re.compile(
    r'(and|with)\s+[A-Za-z_][A-Za-z0-9_.\-]*\s*=', re.IGNORECASE)   # tools.py:431
\end{lstlisting}

Executed against the real parser:

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Executed transcript: a Spanish conjunction silently swallows the remaining arguments into the file path.},label={lst:parser-corruption}]
Input : Crea un archivo con filepath='C:/Temp/a.txt' y content='hola'
Parsed: {'requested_key': 'filepath',
         'value': "C:/Temp/a.txt' y content='hola"}
\end{lstlisting}

This is not a routing miss; it is \textbf{data corruption}. File-Creator would
create a file literally named \code{C:/Temp/a.txt' y content='hola}, and every
subsequent parameter is silently lost. The irony is exact: \code{con} is the
Spanish translation of the \code{with} the parser already handles.

\subsection{A Spanish failure is recorded as a success}
\label{sec:failure-as-success}

The failure classifier reads structured JSON status fields correctly, but for
plain-text results it tests eleven English prefixes (\code{error:},
\code{unable to}, \code{cannot}, \code{permission denied}, \dots). Spanish and
OS-localized equivalents --- \emph{No se puede}, \emph{No se pudo}, \emph{Acceso
denegado}, \emph{Excepción:}, \emph{El sistema no puede encontrar la ruta
especificada} --- match nothing, so the call is scored \textbf{SUCCESS}.

This single classifier feeds the Exec Report verdict, the corrective-feedback
loop, the repetition breaker, \textbf{and the Create-Flow button}, which builds a
downloadable workflow from only the successfully-executed calls. A failed
Spanish-locale step is therefore silently baked into a saved workflow as a
working node.

\subsection{Two failures with no Spanish prompt involved}
\label{sec:os-locale-failures}

Monitor Netstat runs \code{netstat -an} with no codepage forcing and greps for
\code{LISTENING}; a Spanish Windows prints \textbf{\code{ESCUCHANDO}}. And
Forker, Raiser and Stopper perform case-sensitive substring matching of
user-authored patterns against logs with no ``pattern never matched'' warning, so
a mismatched pattern causes the flow to hang silently in its polling loop. Both
are \textbf{operating-system locale} bugs, independent of the user's language.

\subsection{What already survives --- and why it matters}
\label{sec:what-survives}

The safety, audit and deduplication layer is \textbf{language-neutral by
construction}, verified explicitly: the Ask-Execs permission allowlist, the
Exec-Report map, shell inference, the deduplication signature and the wrapped-run
status are all keyed on \emph{tool names}, JSON argument keys and exit codes,
never on prose. The Parametrizer's \code{INI\_SECTION} grammar is
ASCII-structural, reads logs as UTF-8 with \code{errors='replace'}, and writes
YAML with \code{allow\_unicode=True}.

This bounds the blast radius precisely, and it is the most important positive
finding in the study:

\begin{keyinsight}
A Spanish operator already keeps the permission gate and the audit trail. What
they lose is having the correct tool selected in the first place.
\end{keyinsight}

The failure is one of \emph{routing and parsing}, not of \emph{safety} --- which
is exactly why a verification-based algorithm can repair it.

\subsection{Three neutralization operators, with an identity guarantee}
\label{sec:neutralization-operators}

The intuitive repair --- ``add Spanish keywords everywhere'' --- produces a
\textbf{bilingual} core: roughly 2{,}389 lexical items multiplied by every future
language, every new agent requiring translated hints, and the tuned English
behaviour perturbed on every edit. We reject it in favour of a
\textbf{language-neutral} core built from three operators, each of which is
provably the identity on ASCII English input.

\begin{description}
  \item[N1 --- Folding.] NFKD-normalize, drop combining marks, lowercase, then
  apply the \emph{existing} token rule. \code{código} \textrightarrow{}
  \code{codigo}, \code{análisis} \textrightarrow{} \code{analisis}. On pure
  ASCII, NFKD is the identity and ASCII carries no combining marks, so the output
  is byte-identical to today's. This restores the token-overlap term and
  eliminates the accent-dependent nondeterminism of \S\ref{sec:tokenizer}.

  \item[N2 --- Boundary-aware phrase matching.] Replace substring containment
  with boundary-anchored matching, falling back to plain containment when the
  phrase's own edges are non-alphanumeric (so multi-word and punctuated hints
  such as \code{--noreload} are unaffected). This eliminates the entire class of
  \S\ref{sec:tool-selection} collisions. \textbf{It is independently an English
  fix}: \code{api} inside \emph{rapid} and \code{ls} inside \emph{false} are
  English collisions that exist today.

  \item[N3 --- Canonical-key expansion.] A data-only lexicon maps non-English
  intent terms to hint tokens \textbf{that already exist in the registry},
  enforced by a closure test, and appends them to the scored text. Because no new
  hint is ever invented, the scorer's tuned English behaviour remains the only
  behaviour; the Spanish request is simply \emph{lifted into the canonical key
  space} before scoring.
\end{description}

\begin{lemma}[Identity Lemma]
\label{lem:identity}
For any pure-ASCII input, $\mathrm{N1} \circ \mathrm{N2} \circ \mathrm{N3}$ is
the identity transformation on the scoring result.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$\mathrm{N1}$ is the identity on ASCII by the argument above. $\mathrm{N2}$ can
only ever \emph{remove} a match --- it never introduces one --- and every English
hint that matched at a word boundary continues to match; the removals it performs
on English are exactly the accidental collisions listed above, which must
therefore be re-baselined explicitly rather than silently. $\mathrm{N3}$
short-circuits to the identity when $\ell = \textsf{en}$.
\end{proof}

The lemma is enforced empirically by a golden corpus of \textasciitilde{}200
English prompts asserting byte-identical scores before and after --- and the N2
exception is handled honestly: the small number of English scores that
\emph{change} are the collisions being repaired, and each is reviewed
individually rather than waved through.

Alongside these, three further repairs belong to the same pass and are all
\textbf{language-independent bug fixes}: the failure classifier gains a localized
\emph{positive} branch without altering its ``unknown $\Rightarrow$ success''
default (\S\ref{sec:failure-as-success}); the argument parser gains a
quote-masking pre-pass before any conjunction widening
(\S\ref{sec:conjunction-corruption}); and locale-sensitive child processes are
spawned with an invariant environment and decoded explicitly
(\S\ref{sec:os-locale-failures}).

\begin{keyinsight}
\textbf{Sequencing constraint (non-negotiable).} Emitting Spanish text from pool
agents while the eleven English failure prefixes still decide success would make
a Spanish error score SUCCESS and place a failing agent into a saved workflow.
The classifier fix and any Spanish seeding must land in the \textbf{same commit},
or neither.
\end{keyinsight}

\subsection{Why this section precedes the algorithm}
\label{sec:why-substrate-first}

NEPANTLA's guarantee rests on a verifier and a ladder. Both assume the
deterministic core is a \emph{function of intent}, not a function of \emph{the
language intent happens to be expressed in}. \S\ref{sec:substrate} establishes
that this assumption does not currently hold, and
\S\ref{sec:neutralization-operators} establishes how to make it hold without
perturbing English. Only then is the ladder well-defined.
