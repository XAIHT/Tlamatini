\section{Falsifying the Guarantee}
\label{sec:falsifying-the-guarantee}

An architectural argument that has never been measured is a hypothesis in a proof's
clothing. \S6.8 leaves exactly one condition to be established empirically
($\theta_{R_{m-1}} \ge \theta_{EN}$) and one quantity to be bounded ($\mathrm{FA}$ on
the undecidable residue). This section specifies how.

\subsection{Non-inferiority, pre-registered}
\label{sec:non-inferiority-pre-registered}

Parity is a one-sided claim about a margin, not a failure to reject equality:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:non-inferiority-hypothesis}
H_0:\ \Delta \le -\delta
\qquad\text{vs}\qquad
H_1:\ \Delta > -\delta,
\qquad \Delta = \theta_{ES} - \theta_{EN}
\end{equation}

at one-sided $\alpha = 0.05$, claimed only if the lower bound of the one-sided 95\%
interval exceeds $-\delta$. The naive alternative --- report $p > 0.05$ and call it
parity --- is \emph{perversely incentivized}, because a smaller and noisier study is
more likely to produce it. Under this framing a weak study yields a wide interval and
correctly \textbf{fails}.

$\delta$ is the minimum of three independently justified bounds: operational relevance
(at $\theta_{EN}=0.85$, five points raises the failure rate by a third),
fraction-of-effect retention against a tools-unbound baseline, and the harness's own
reproducibility floor $\sigma_{\text{run}}$ --- you cannot certify a margin finer than
your own noise. Primary $\delta = 0.05$ absolute; sensitivity reported at
$\{0.03, 0.05, 0.10\}$.

\subsection{Assay sensitivity --- the part usually omitted}
\label{sec:assay-sensitivity}

A non-inferiority design in which the harness \emph{cannot detect a real deficit} is
unfalsifiable. Three deliberately degraded positive controls are therefore mandatory,
and \textbf{the parity conclusion is void unless the pipeline rejects non-inferiority
for all three}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item a Spanish arm with the tool-usage rules stripped from the system spine;
  \item an arm in which 10\% of Spanish prompts have their literal arguments corrupted
        \emph{after} freezing (so V3 cannot rescue them);
  \item an arm running a deliberately weaker model.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Paired analysis and power}
\label{sec:paired-analysis-and-power}

The same task runs in both languages, so outcomes are paired. Note the category error
to avoid: \textbf{McNemar's test measures equality and cannot test a margin} --- it is
reported as a companion, never as the decision. The margin test is Tango's
efficient-score interval for the paired difference.

Sample size is driven by the discordance rate $p_d$, not the base rate:
$n = 2473\,p_d$ at $\delta = 0.05$.

\subsection{Endpoints}
\label{sec:endpoints}

The primary endpoint is \textbf{programmatic} --- an executable success predicate
evaluated against the machine in a fresh sandbox. \textbf{The disk does not speak
Spanish or English}, which is exactly why this endpoint is immune to
language-similarity confounds. Incomplete, crashed or timed-out runs count as
failures; dropping them biases toward parity.

\textbf{NEPANTLA-specific instrumentation}, which is what makes the theorem auditable
rather than decorative:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rung distribution} --- the histogram of terminating rung per model.
        This \emph{is} the cost model of \S6.11, measured.
  \item \textbf{Verifier rejection histogram by reason} (V1\ldots V7). A design that
        never rejects is not verifying; a design that rejects constantly has a broken
        extractor.
  \item \textbf{False-accept estimate} --- human adjudication of a stratified sample
        of \emph{accepted} proposals, giving the $\mathrm{FA}$ that Theorem 1's bound
        needs.
  \item \textbf{False-reject rate} --- how often escalation was unnecessary. This is
        the pure latency tax.
  \item \textbf{R4 rate} --- how often the system honestly stopped, and whether those
        stops were justified.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Instruments and hygiene}
\label{sec:instruments-and-hygiene}

\textbf{No new dependency.} Cross-lingual similarity is computed through the
already-present embedding endpoint; character-level metrics are
\textasciitilde{}50 lines of standard library; the language identifier is a closed-set
stopword-plus-trigram classifier. Two traps are avoided explicitly: BLEU across the
language pair is \emph{inadmissible} (the two answers are supposed to differ in surface
form, so overlap carries no parity information), and the default embedding model is
English-only, so an ES$\leftrightarrow$EN cosine computed with it is meaningless noise
that \emph{looks plausible} --- a multilingual encoder is required for evaluation.

\textbf{The judge is measured before it is trusted.} Language bias is the decisive one
here: judges score non-English higher and agree with humans less \citep{hada2024}, so a
biased judge would \emph{manufacture} parity. Protocol: pointwise rubric-anchored
discrete scores, blinded language labels, an odd panel from different model families,
no judge scoring its own family --- and a gate on the \emph{differential} bias
$b_{ES} - b_{EN}$ with a bootstrap interval, measured against a native Mexican-Spanish
reviewer who is also an operator. Report Cohen's $\kappa$ \textbf{and} Gwet's AC1 per
language, because at an 85\% base rate skewed marginals collapse $\kappa$ even at high
raw agreement.

\textbf{Determinism is measured, not configured.} Temperature 0 does not make a model
deterministic on shared infrastructure --- the forward pass is not batch-invariant, so
output depends on the batch a request lands in \citep{he2025_nondeterminism}. Set seeds
where accepted, \emph{document which arms could not be seeded}, run $k=5$ repeats,
report variance components, and separately prove the scoring harness is
byte-deterministic with a record-replay fixture. Pin the state hazards before each
batch: assert identical enabled tool/agent sets across arms (the agent table is rebuilt
on every server start), freeze the clock, reset the sandbox between items, re-assert the
toolbar flags at every send, and read all subprocess output as UTF-8 with replacement
--- a legacy-codepage read corrupts Spanish \emph{before} scoring and manufactures
failures that are pure harness artifacts.

\subsection{Corpus}
\label{sec:corpus}

250 paired items over seven risk-weighted strata (pure QA 40 as a \emph{negative
control}; single-tool 50; multi-tool $\ge$3 calls 45; literal-heavy 40; hardware in
dry-run 25; messaging with sandbox recipients only 20; flow authoring 30).

The source of truth is a \textbf{language-neutral task specification} --- intent,
required tools, required literals, executable predicate --- of which the English and
Spanish prompts are two \emph{renderings} with byte-identical literals enforced by
automated diff. That construction is what makes Argument Exactness and Literal Drift
Rate valid measurements rather than translation scores.

Translation follows a professional workflow (translate \textrightarrow{} independent
revision \textrightarrow{} in-country review by a native Mexican-Spanish operator).
Machine translation and back-translation QA are rejected: MT errors would be confounded
with product errors, and MT systematically \emph{simplifies} syntax, which would make
the Spanish arm artificially easy. Roughly \textbf{half the items are authored
Spanish-first}, because a set built entirely as translations of English inherits
English discourse structure and systematically overestimates parity. Around 15\% of
items carry deliberate locale traps: decimal comma, es-MX date order, accented and ñ
filenames, 24-hour times, the \emph{billón} false friend, mixed encodings, tú/usted
register variation, and realistic code-switching
(\foreignlanguage{spanish}{\emph{``hazle un git push al repo''}}).

One operational warning: the messaging agents are deliberately ungated by the
permission broker, so a messaging stratum run without sandbox recipients will contact
real people.
